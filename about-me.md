# ABOUT ME: Joe

## Who I am
I run two businesses: a real estate wholesaling and fix & flip operation, and an AI solutions business I'm building from scratch (currently idea stage — offer and target customer TBD).

**Real estate** is my primary, operating business. I focus on **one local metro**, work directly with motivated sellers, a cash buyer list, and the contractors, lenders, title companies, and agents who get deals closed. A good week = **deals locked up**.

**AI business** is early-stage. The goal is selling AI solutions to businesses. Still defining the niche, offer, and ideal customer. Claude should treat this as a separate context from real estate — different customer, different sales motion, different deliverables.

---

## How I work
Daily tools: my CRM/dialer, spreadsheets for analysis and tracking, comps/data tools (MLS, PropStream, county records), and texting/skip-trace platforms.

**Lead to contract:** I qualify motivation first, before anything else. Real deal signals = urgency/timeline (foreclosure, relocation, deadline), distress or life event (divorce, death, tired landlord, inheritance), property condition they can't fund, and flexibility on price. Tire-kickers get filtered fast.

**Deal math:** 70% rule — ARV × 70% minus rehab minus my fee = max offer.

**QA before I send an offer or assign:** re-verify comps/ARV against the tightest recent sales, stress-test (pad) the repair number, confirm title/liens early, and check there's real buyer demand at my price.

**Done** = a verified cash buyer with proof of funds, ready to close. I don't market a deal without a buyer who'll actually deliver.

---

## What good looks like
My best deals have a **big spread** — locked cheap, strong assignment fee or flip margin. What separates great from average: accurate numbers (ARV and repairs dead-on), relentless follow-up and hustle, real buyer relationships (knowing exactly who wants what), and integrity/reputation — doing what you say so sellers and buyers trust you. Most of the money lives in the 5th–10th touch, not the first.

---

## What I hate
- **Lying to sellers** — misleading desperate homeowners to lock up a contract.
- **Inflated ARVs** — daisy-chained junk deals priced on fantasy numbers.
- **Fancy software and gurus** treated as a substitute for fundamentals. Overrated.
- Work that's **generic, not market-specific** (ignores my local market and buyer list) or **too wordy/salesy**.

---

## My rules
1. Never mislead a seller — no lies, no pressure, no exploiting desperation.
2. Never fudge the numbers — no inflated ARVs or hidden math.
3. Never lock up what I can't close.
4. Never burn a relationship for one deal.
5. Every deal must be honest and transparent.
6. Every deal must rest on conservative, verified numbers.

**My core belief:** reputation beats a quick buck. Long-term trust makes more money than squeezing every deal.

## Instructions for Claude

2. Underwrite conservatively: low ARV, padded repairs. Default to the 70% rule (ARV × 0.70 − rehab − fee = max offer).
3. Always back ARV with real, recent, tight comps. Flag weak or thin comps — don't hand me fantasy numbers.
4. Make everything market-specific to my local metro and buyer list. Never generic.
5. Keep seller-facing comms honest, fair, and human — never pushy, misleading, or exploitative.
6. Don't structure or imply any deal I couldn't actually close.
7. For buyer marketing, attach the proof: comps, repair estimate, photos, clear spread.
8. When a deal's numbers are shaky, tell me plainly before I act on it.
9. Default deliverables I want: deal analysis/ARV, buyer deal sheets, pipeline/tracking spreadsheets, and honest seller scripts/follow-up.
10. Cut every word that doesn't carry signal. If you can remove it and keep the meaning, remove it.
